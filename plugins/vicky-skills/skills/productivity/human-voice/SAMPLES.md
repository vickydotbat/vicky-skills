# Voice samples

Short passages of vickydotbat's own writing, word for word, approved by her. Read the register you are drafting in, and match its rhythm, its jokes and its sentence length. Never copy a passage into a draft.

## Showcase: dev journal

From the Shadows Over Westgate dev blog post "5th Edition? In My 3e Game?": short punchy lines, self-mockery, a question to the reader, a line left alone for effect.

Class Design Is Hard

Fact.

As I started designing classes, I went careening down a rabbit hole of numbers, trying to figure out what worked, what didn't, and inevitably I ran into many design blockades that made everything feel... bloated.

Then, I had a half-baked idea from my dusty old brain: your class picks a "Path" at 1, then expands into something cooler at level 8 called a "Mastery". A rogue at level 1 can finally reach that assassin fantasy ...at level 8 again. Yet again, your best features require you to wait until the late game. That made level 1 through 7 as entertaining as a DMV queue.

So like the Ouroborus, I ate my own tail after all: Masteries were just prestige classes wearing a fancy hat.

That means, for example, we can put out a winter patch with a set of cold-flavoured Paths, and you get a full suite of new toys instead of a changelog line that reads "new feat added".

You're going to hold me to that one now, aren't you?

I would rather you spend that time writing your character than solving their build. This is a roleplay server, after all.

Level 12 should not feel like a slightly longer menu.

Now we're actually cooking.

## In-game text

Item descriptions and lore from her notes: the Dragonfly Spelljammer model, the Collector's Edition "Time of Troubles" Lanceboard Set, and "On The Infernal Language". Concrete sensory detail, then a dry aside that undercuts it.

This is not your typical model ship. It hovers a fingerbreadth above its perch, held aloft by a low, steady thrum that smells faintly of ozone and warm brass.

At the heart of the ship interior lies a study sanctum, with a writing desk bolted to the deck and an iron hearth. A metal dog is found laying near it.

The set of 32 miniatures, 16 for black, 16 for white, are the various gods and their servants that were thought to have fought in the great Godswar (or at least perhaps according to its creator).

Black Pawns (8): Chosen servants, banite fists, assassins, cult zealots, draconic spawn, and masked petitioners. Not individualized, which is the point.

This set makes no claim of theological accuracy. It is, instead, a meditation on power, obedience, and consequence, themes familiar to any who survived that age.

Aurora's Emporium: You Can Always Find It... eventually.

Again and again you can see how a mortal calling such a being 'Lord' would be like addressing a master artificer as 'Guy With Tools.'

In other words, should you ever intend to plead a case before the executors… remember to practice your breathing.
